# Promo film: best practices from the talks, and how Harness Firmware ships them

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: built at `/promo` from the audited copy deck; waiting for the owner's review. No MP4 export until the owner approves the film.

A shareable 16:9 silent film at `/promo` (`site/promo.html`, `site/promo-film.mjs`, `site/promo.css`) for a developer who already uses Claude Code or Codex. It opens on the owner's statement of what Harness Firmware is for, in the owner's words, then three problems and how Harness Firmware answers each, with the person's final yes. Ten quote beats follow. Each sets a best practice from an AI Engineer talk (the practice in plain words, a verbatim excerpt of the speaker's quote, the speaker) beside the mechanism Harness Firmware ships for it, with a picture of that mechanism at work. Three beats then cover things no talk covered, a results beat shows three pull requests from the template's own history, and the close names what comes switched on and where to get it.

Every on-screen line comes word for word from the copy deck `docs/specs/2026-10-12-promo-film-copy.md`. Quotes, timestamps and talk titles come from `docs/promo/social-proof.md`; no other quotes are used. Length: 300.3 s (5:00), 17 beats.

## The standard each beat meets

The owner's standard for the claims is "better than the average setup, and it ships ready". Each Harness Firmware line names a specific mechanism and says it comes built in and switched on, where a stock Claude Code or Codex user would have to find the parts and wire them up. The film never says stock Claude Code or Codex cannot do something, and never claims a mechanism only Harness Firmware has.

Each quote beat and each of the three later beats carries an off-screen pair in the deck: `Stock:` (the stock parts a user would wire up, each with its documentation link) and `Edge:` (the Harness Firmware mechanism, with the template file and section that ships it, tagged `[strong]` or `[ready]`). The reference for both is the audited stock baseline `docs/specs/2026-10-12-promo-film-stock-baseline.md`.

## Rules this film follows

- The open starts with the owner's statement, word for word in three lines (trailing periods dropped by the display rule): "Harness Firmware aims to solve the spikiness of agent quality by steering them to work better than they normally would through reliable and repeatable methods", "Models have basically solved code. They haven't solved reliable verification", "Harness Firmware is an ongoing engineering project to attack that bottleneck". It is the owner's voice, so it is not compressed. It reads over the dust before the machine assembles, then clears for the tagline and the three rows.
- Comparison, not endorsement. Each quote beat has two labelled groups: "Best practice" (the practice, the quote, the speaker) top left and "In Harness Firmware" (tag, answer, how, why) bottom left. Nothing on screen links a speaker to Harness Firmware. Attribution is name and company beside the quote ("Dru Knox · Tessl"), no logos. The open and the close carry a small line, "No speaker reviewed or endorsed Harness Firmware", and the sources list under the film says the same.
- Talk titles never appear on screen. They appear only in the page's sources list under the film, where each quote links to the moment it was said.
- Display copy is compressed for strangers ("caveman ultra", the owner's choice for this film): fragments with no articles or filler, arrows for cause and sequence, only abbreviations a developer already knows (PR, CI, repo), while each line still names who does what to which file.
- Never claim that no pull request can merge without your approval. Owner's setup items (weekly-review, disk-cleanup, token guard, Harness Console, the talk-by-talk comparison) are not shown.
- The human step appears wherever the loop is described: you give the final yes (open, close); you own each decision, can veto an assumption and pick Go (p1); you start the long job (p2); you type `/merge` (g1); you review the rule-edit pull request, which never merges itself (i1); you merge or type `/merge` (x1); an item the two chats cannot settle comes to you (x2).
- Display text has no closing periods and no em dashes. Quotes keep their own punctuation.
- Reading rule: each text element stays on screen until its beat ends, and the beat lasts long enough to read it and everything revealed after it at 3 words a second, plus 1 s, plus a 0.55 s fade. A readout replaced by a later one gets its own reading time plus 1.3 s. The one exception is the open's statement, which clears before the tagline: its three lines stay until all of it has been read at 3 words a second plus about 1 s, then fade, and the tagline starts after the fade. Reveal times, the statement's clear time and beat lengths are computed from word counts, never set by hand.
- The picture is part of the claim. Whatever a readout states is true in the machine for as long as the readout shows, the machine keeps acting while the captions describe action, and nothing from the next beat appears before that beat starts.
